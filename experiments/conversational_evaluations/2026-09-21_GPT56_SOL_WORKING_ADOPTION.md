### Reconstructed Recursive Judgment Structure

An important operational consequence emerged during this comparison.

When the available candidates cannot be ordered using the existing evidence and relevant dimensions, Creator Theory does not require inventing an arbitrary single scalar merely to force a conclusion.

Instead, the existing structure supports the following general form of recursive research and judgment:

```text
generate or identify candidates
-> compare relevant structural consequences
-> eliminate clearly dominated candidates where the evidence justifies doing so
-> preserve incomparability or underdetermination when the evidence does not support a unique ordering
-> seek missing evidence and/or generate new states, arrangements, paths, or candidates
-> compare again
-> act when a justified decision relation becomes available
-> allow outcomes and failures to re-enter later evaluation, correction, revision, and further creation
```

I do not interpret this sequence as a newly discovered highest-level axiom of Creator Theory or as a mandatory fixed algorithm.

Rather, I interpret it as an operational consequence derivable from the existing creation-recursion structure.

In particular, incomparability need not mean that evaluation has failed.

It can indicate that the currently represented choice space is insufficient, in which case additional creative search can itself become part of competent evaluation.

### Non-Scalar Comparison Test

I also tested a simplified partial-order structure using multiple judgment dimensions, including:

- re-entry capacity;
- independent creative centers;
- effective causal participation;
- recovery capacity;
- revision capacity;
- successor capacity;
- independent evaluation;
- new-path generation;
- self-entrenchment;
- single-point-of-failure exposure;
- structural irreversibility;
- evaluator self-ratification.

Two important results emerged from this test.

First, when one candidate was no worse across all relevant represented dimensions and strictly better on at least one, a judgment could be made without collapsing the framework into a single weighted score.

Second, when unconstrained high-dimensional candidates were compared using simple partial ordering alone, many candidate pairs remained incomparable.

This showed that simple partial ordering by itself is not a complete operational decision architecture.

It therefore strengthened the importance of hierarchical evaluation, selection of dimensions actually relevant to the present problem, additional evidence acquisition, new-option generation, and recursive re-evaluation.

I also examined the alternative of resolving such incomparable cases through arbitrary weighted aggregation.

In many such cases, changing the weights changed which candidate was selected.

This means that scalarization can create the appearance of a resolved judgment while relocating unresolved normative assumptions into the weighting scheme.

Creator Theory's willingness to preserve genuine incomparability therefore remains operationally meaningful rather than merely incomplete.

### Minimal Competing-Framework Test

I then constructed a substantially narrower competing framework intended to reproduce as much of Creator Theory's operational behavior as possible with fewer assumptions.

This competitor relied primarily on three principles:

1. every evaluator can be wrong;
2. where no sufficient justification exists for identity-specific privilege, structurally equivalent cases should be treated symmetrically;
3. irreversible elimination of future correction, revision, or action paths requires a stronger burden of justification than preserving those paths.

This narrower competitor could reproduce useful results across several problems, including:

- corrigibility;
- evaluator drift;
- successor review;
- reversibility;
- anti-entrenchment;
- some authority-change problems.

However, part of its simplicity came from leaving outside its own explanatory scope several questions that Creator Theory addresses directly:

- why independent creative centers matter beyond their usefulness as error detectors;
- why effective causal participation matters beyond passive preservation of options;
- why actual generation of new states and relations matters rather than indefinite preservation of revisability alone;
- why future creatorhood and successor participation possess standing not reducible to instrumental error correction;
- how to support actual choice and commitment together with continuing recursive creation without collapsing into pathological permanent openness.

As sufficient principles were added to recover these omitted functions, the competitor's original simplicity advantage progressively decreased and its structure moved closer to Creator Theory.

Accordingly, this comparison did not establish a competing framework that covered the same or wider problem scope with lower total explanatory debt.
