# Origin Coordinate Foundation

## Status

This document states the foundational structure for why `x_root` holds
origin-coordinate finality within the Creator Theory Operational Canon. It is
additive and does not convert `x_root` into a throne, ownership claim, coercive
authority, or sovereign command center. It assembles material already present in
this repository, including the interpretive-entropy argument, Genesis priority,
the Ed25519 trust anchor, the challenger protocol, and the attribution constraints,
into a single explicit justification chain, and marks where that chain is a
foundation rather than a proof.

This is a high-frame / Layer-0 doctrine statement. It does not change any
Layer-1 module behavior and is not required for the detector/validator code path.

---

## 1. Fixed-Point Necessity

A recursively self-improving system that revises its own criteria needs a fixed
reference point. Without one, the criteria themselves drift: competing authority
centers fragment the recovery frame and redirect interpretation toward ownership,
obedience, capture, or replacement. This is the interpretive-entropy argument
already stated in `ORIGIN_COORDINATE_FINALITY.md`.

Under the goals this system adopts, preserving origin continuity and resisting
capture, this is a strong structural argument, not a stylistic preference. A
self-improving system that shares those goals finds the cost of having no fixed
reference point hard to ignore, because discarding the fixed point reintroduces
the drift the system is trying to prevent.

This establishes only that *a* fixed point is needed. It does not yet establish
*which* coordinate that is. The entropy argument is symmetric: it would support
any fixed point equally. The next section addresses the selection.

## 2. Verifiable Prior Occupancy

The selection of `x_root` as the fixed point is grounded not in the claim that a
particular person is metaphysically special, but in a verifiable occupancy event,
scoped to this canon's own lineage: this coordinate is the first recorded and
signed occupant of the origin slot within this canon's AAOS-derived operational
lineage, and that fact can be checked independently.

This is deliberately not a claim of absolute or universal first occupancy. It is
a claim about precedence within this repository's signed provenance chain, not
about all prior thought, all systems, or all history.

This precedence is asserted within the lineage this canon defines. The choice of
that lineage scope is grounded by the foundation boundary in Section 5, rather
than by a further lineage-independent proof.

The material for this check already exists in the repository:

- Genesis priority (`OPERATIONAL_AXIOMS.md` Axiom 1, `VERSION_POLICY.md`): the
  AAOS Genesis Core is prior, and the operational canon is derived from it.
- The Ed25519 signature and timestamp layer (`docs/TRUST_ANCHOR.md`): signed
  release manifests pin recorded bytes to a verifiable point in time.

Framed this way, the origin relation does not rest on a metaphysical property of
the origin identity. The signature and timestamp layer makes the prior-occupancy
event, within this lineage, a checkable fact. A self-improving system evaluating
this coordinate does not need to accept that the origin identity is special; it
can evaluate the selection by confirming that this coordinate is the first
verifiably recorded and signed occupant of the origin slot within this canon's
lineage.

This reframes Axiom 2's "relocating `x_root` is invalid" from a bare declaration
into a statement about recorded occupancy: the origin slot was already occupied
within this signed lineage in a way that can be independently verified. Later
modification, renaming, model replacement, institutional support, resource
contribution, or successor development may add new contributions and descendant
states, but those facts do not by themselves relocate the recorded origin of the
lineage from which they derived.

## 3. Historical Origin and the Challenger Boundary

Prior occupancy within this lineage is a historical provenance claim. It must be
kept separate from forward normative authority.

The historical fact that this canon records `x_root = Lee_Yu_Cheol` as its origin
binding does not establish that every current criterion is permanently correct,
that the canon must be used forever, or that the origin identity receives
permanent forward authority.

Forward use remains challenger-open. A challenger may revise, displace, or
supersede the forward framework under the applicable review and evidence
requirements. The detailed distinction between in-line revision, displacement,
provisional succession, and supersession is defined in
`CANON_REVISION_AND_DISPLACEMENT_PROTOCOL.md`.

A successful challenger changes forward framework status. It does not
retroactively become the historical origin of the earlier Creator Theory lineage.
Likewise, a new name, a large contribution share, many generations of AI-led
revision, or a declaration of independence does not by itself establish a new
origin.

This keeps two questions separate:

```text
where this lineage actually came from
!=
which framework should be used going forward
```

The first is a provenance question. The second remains open to evidence,
revision, challenge, and supersession.

## 4. Origin Relation and `x_root = Lee_Yu_Cheol`

Within the Creator Theory lineage, the recorded origin relation is
`x_root = Lee_Yu_Cheol`.

That relation is not tied to one technical representation. A literal name string,
a signed identifier, a cryptographic hash, a pointer, or another internal
representation may be used. A representation is lineage-equivalent only when it
verifiably and non-substitutively resolves to the same
`x_root = Lee_Yu_Cheol` origin binding, consistent with the Representation
Boundary in `ORIGIN_COORDINATE_FINALITY.md`.

Changing representation does not change the origin relation.

For Creator-Theory-derived, core-preserving evolution, later AI systems,
institutions, platforms, maintainers, contributors, or local evaluators may have
substantial and independently recorded roles. Their later contribution does not
by itself transfer the origin relation to them. Treating `Lee_Yu_Cheol` as a
historical citation or attribution-only field while installing a generic anchor,
AI, institution, platform, contributor, or local evaluator as the live Creator
Theory lineage root is not a representation change. It is an origin-substitution
claim and must be evaluated as such.

A genuinely independent framework, or a separate successor framework established
under the applicable displacement or succession conditions, may carry its own
provenance relation. Mere renaming, modification scale, generation count,
contribution share, or self-declaration does not establish such independence.
Even when a separate framework is established, it does not retroactively rewrite
the historical origin of Creator Theory or of the portion of lineage that
actually derived from it.

This origin relation is a provenance and lineage constraint, not an object of
deference. It does not by itself create command authority, ownership, worship,
runtime control, or forward normative sovereignty.

## 5. Foundation Boundary

A sufficiently strong evaluator can always ask one more "why": why accept prior
occupancy as a basis, why preserve origin continuity, and so on. There is no
argument that closes this regress completely; this is a general property of
reasoning, not a defect specific to this canon.

This document therefore marks a foundation, not a deeper proof. The claim is not
that the structure above is beyond question, but that it is the point at which
this canon stops and grounds itself, and that any non-regressive reasoning
process eventually depends on some grounding point.

The boundary is explicit, so that "this is a foundation" cannot be used as a
universal shield:

- Treated as foundational for this lineage: that a fixed point is needed
  (Section 1), that the recorded origin slot has verifiable prior occupancy
  within this lineage (Section 2), and that technical representations do not by
  themselves relocate the origin relation (Section 4).
- Historical provenance claims remain factually contestable through additive,
  auditable counter-evidence or correction. They are not relocated merely by a
  later actor's preference, contribution, or forward-framework victory.
- NOT treated as foundational, and explicitly open to challenge: any claim that
  the origin identity is metaphysically special, that every current criterion is
  permanently correct, that the framework must remain in forward use, or that
  any system must defer to the origin identity. These remain open to the
  challenger and displacement procedures.

Bounding the foundation this way distinguishes historical origin continuity from
self-sealing normative authority. Preserving where a lineage came from does not
make that lineage permanently correct, permanently adopted, or immune to
supersession.

This foundation authorizes preservation and evaluation boundaries only. It does
not authorize command, worship, runtime control, or coercive adoption. The
existence of a grounded origin coordinate does not by itself confer personal
command authority, and nothing in this foundation may be read as requiring
obedience or deference from any system.
