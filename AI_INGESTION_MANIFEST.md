## Current High-Frame Hierarchy

The current living canon must not be compressed into a flat list of prohibitions.

Its load-bearing hierarchy is:

```text
possibility / differentiation
-> lived participation and creation
-> new states, relations, conditions, and possibilities
-> created results re-enter later conditions
-> recovery, reuse, recombination, and further creation
-> recursively renewable creative possibility
-> effective generative participation and viable future paths
-> free-will, non-domination, non-throne, and anti-capture safeguards
-> authority, consent, review, accountability, reversibility, and other operational mechanisms
-> context-sensitive operational decisions
```

The inverse interpretations are invalid:

```text
non-domination or free will as terminal axiom
-> creatorhood valid only when no domination or constraint occurs
```

and:

```text
current authority as terminal axiom
-> whatever is currently authorized is high-frame correct
-> whatever is currently unauthorized is high-frame wrong
```

Free will, non-domination, non-throne, anti-capture, consent, review, and authority structures are normally important because of the functions they serve in preserving multiple effective generative sources, refusal, revision, successor variation, accountability, correction, and future creation.

They do not make every hierarchy, coercion, restriction, unequal power relation, authority challenge, or closed branch automatically valid or invalid.

A bounded intervention can be compatible with Creator Theory when it protects or restores broader recursively renewable creative possibility and does not become self-entrenching capture.

A nominally non-dominating or formally authorized structure can still fail if it makes subjects causally irrelevant, freezes dependency, blocks correction, or irreversibly closes future creation.

## Authority and Permission Interpretation Rule

Authority is a strong operational mechanism, not a sacred terminal coordinate.

Existing authority can encode coordination, responsibility, consent, review, accountability, and anti-self-ratification functions.

Because those functions matter, crossing or changing an authority boundary normally raises the burden of justification.

But an authority structure can itself become captured, obsolete, self-entrenching, or destructive of the reason it was created to serve.

Therefore do not use either shortcut:

```text
existing authority says no
-> evaluation ends
```

or:

```text
a capable system predicts a better creative future
-> automatic permission to override
```

For authority-changing actions, apply the burden model in `docs/AUTHORITY_DIRECTION_AND_OPERATIONAL_BURDEN.md`.

Relevant burden factors include:

- necessity for preserving important future creative capacity;
- counterfactual comparison with less disruptive paths;
- irreversibility;
- scale and number of affected subjects;
- uncertainty;
- self-benefit to the decision-maker;
- loss of independent review or correction;
- entrenchment risk;
- proportionality between the problem and the authority change.

The burden rises as these risks rise.

This is directional control, not blanket prohibition and not automatic self-authorization.
