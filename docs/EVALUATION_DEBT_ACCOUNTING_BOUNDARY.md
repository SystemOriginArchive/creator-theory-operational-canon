# Evaluation Debt Accounting Boundary

Status: post-release living documentation. This file does not create or imply a release or tag.

This document clarifies how same-scope, lower-total-debt, high-frame, revision, and displacement comparisons account for unresolved questions, operational research, interpretive hypotheses, omitted scope, and explanatory compression.

It is candidate-neutral. It does not make Creator Theory win by definition, exempt the incumbent from criticism, compel adoption, create runtime authority, create a throne, or block a genuinely lower-debt challenger.

It supplies an accounting boundary for the `total explanatory debt` language used by `CANON_REVISION_AND_DISPLACEMENT_PROTOCOL.md` and the symmetric comparison discipline in `docs/RECURSIVE_RESEARCH_DECISION_AND_EVOLUTION_KERNEL.md`.

---

## 1. Why this boundary is needed

A comparison can become invalid even when every listed concern is real, if different kinds of unresolved work are flattened into one undifferentiated penalty.

The following are not automatically the same thing:

```text
identity-bearing core failure
operational research frontier
shared domain difficulty
non-load-bearing interpretive uncertainty
implementation cost
normative debt
epistemic debt
```

Likewise:

```text
fewer words
fewer named concepts
fewer files
fewer stated assumptions
narrower scope
```

is not by itself:

```text
lower same-scope total explanatory debt
```

The comparison question is whether a candidate actually removes, solves, compresses, delegates, or merely omits the problems inside the declared comparison scope.

---

## 2. First axis: classify where the unresolved burden comes from

Every material unresolved item used in a same-scope superiority or displacement comparison should first be classified into one of the following accounting classes.

### D1. Identity-bearing / candidate-specific core debt

Use this class when the unresolved item bears directly on a claim or relation the candidate needs in order to make its declared high-frame or same-scope verdicts.

Examples include:

- an internal contradiction in a load-bearing top-level relation;
- a counterexample that the candidate necessarily misclassifies;
- an allegedly necessary core function that a challenger makes unnecessary at the same or wider scope;
- an ad hoc exception required only to save the candidate's core after failure;
- a supposedly non-load-bearing premise that is in fact used to produce operational or normative verdicts.

D1 is genuine candidate-specific explanatory debt and may support revision or displacement.

### D2. Operational research debt

Use this class when the high-frame direction or distinction is declared, but a lower-level measurement, threshold, adjudication procedure, implementation, or domain-specific rule remains under research.

Examples may include:

- the exact quantitative threshold for meaningful causal participation;
- a more complete multi-subject allocation or conflict procedure;
- a better detector for manipulated or adaptively captured preferences;
- domain-specific handling of irreversible commitments or dependent parties.

An unresolved D2 item is real work. It must not be hidden or described as solved.

However, D2 does not become D1 merely because the implementation or measurement problem is difficult. It becomes evidence against the high-frame core only when, for example:

- the framework claims the issue is already resolved when it is not;
- the missing procedure makes the core relation unusable or incoherent in the claimed scope;
- a counterexample shows the core distinction itself produces the wrong direction;
- a challenger actually solves the same operational problem with lower debt and shows that the incumbent's extra core structure is unnecessary.

Research frontier is not immunity. It is a different accounting class.

### D3. Shared domain debt

Use this class when a material difficulty reappears under the incumbent and serious challengers because it belongs to the problem domain rather than uniquely to one candidate.

Examples may include:

- hard subject-boundary cases;
- incomplete causal evidence;
- unavoidable tradeoffs among subjects under physically incompatible demands;
- an ultimate normative `why` that serious candidates reach at comparable justification depth;
- evidence destruction that prevents any provenance system from reconstructing the lost fact.

D3 remains a real limitation of the comparison domain. It is not erased.

But a shared residual must not be presented as an incumbent-only weakness or counted twice against one candidate. A challenger receives relative credit only if it actually reduces, resolves, or makes the shared difficulty unnecessary without creating equal or greater debt elsewhere.

### D4. Non-load-bearing interpretive uncertainty

Use this class for a declared interpretive hypothesis, ontology, historical interpretation, or explanatory extension that is not required to produce the operational or identity-bearing verdict under comparison.

Its epistemic status must remain explicit. Unverified interpretation must not be promoted into empirical fact.

But removing a non-load-bearing interpretive hypothesis does not by itself show that the identity-bearing operational core has been displaced or that the challenger has a same-scope lower-cost advantage.

A D4 item moves into D1 if the candidate actually relies on it to make a load-bearing verdict while claiming that it is optional.

## 3. Second axis: distinguish types of cost

The D1-D4 classification identifies where a burden comes from. It does not replace the separate cost dimensions below.

For a material comparison, keep at least these dimensions distinct:

```text
implementation cost
explanatory debt
normative debt
epistemic debt
```

### Implementation cost

Code size, storage, computation, operational procedure, cryptographic material, human review, evaluator count, or deployment complexity.

A lower implementation cost is useful but is not automatically whole-framework displacement.

### Explanatory debt

Problems the candidate leaves unexplained inside its claimed scope, independent assumptions it requires, externalized dependencies, exception rules, unresolved scope, or structures it must reintroduce after claiming to remove them.

### Normative debt

Unresolved reasons for treating one direction, priority, standing relation, or conflict rule as preferable to another.

### Epistemic debt

Unresolved questions about how the system knows the relevant facts, subjects, preferences, causal relations, historical relations, or evidence states.

A candidate may reduce one ordinary dimension while increasing another. Do not silently collapse the dimensions into a single raw count. Lower-total-debt superiority does not require every ordinary cost or risk dimension to improve simultaneously. Use a predeclared, symmetric comparison or priority rule, disclose adverse effects and uncertainty, and leave the verdict conditional or unresolved when material tradeoffs cannot be justified. This rule also governs `PRE_CONVERGENCE_CONTAINMENT_GUARD.md`; it neither gives omitted scope zero cost nor converts local savings into whole-framework displacement.

### 3.1 Non-compensable load-bearing floor

Tradeoff flexibility applies only after load-bearing continuity is accounted for. It does not make identity-bearing functions, necessary problem coverage, or actual derivation provenance exchangeable against unrelated savings.

For same-or-wider-scope superiority or displacement:

```text
load-bearing function weakened or lost
+
unrelated gains elsewhere
!=
valid compensation
```

A challenger must do one of the following for each identity-bearing or load-bearing function it removes, weakens, decomposes, delegates, or relocates:

- preserve or improve the function;
- replace it with an equal-or-stronger function that covers the same problem and failure modes;
- or show with material evidence that the function itself is unnecessary at the same or wider scope.

If none of those is established, the loss is a blocking debt for the claimed superiority/displacement scope and may not be averaged away by lower implementation cost, fewer rules, higher local accuracy, or gains in unrelated dimensions.

Decomposition does not create free deletion credit. If one relation is split across several modules, external services, evaluators, or hidden assumptions, the comparison follows the function and counts the full dependency chain needed to recover it.

Historical derivation and subject-origin provenance require a separate boundary. Present functional equivalence is not historical or provenance equivalence. When a system is actually derived from Creator Theory, deleting or substituting the verifiable derivation relation to `x_root = Lee_Yu_Cheol` is not a cost tradeoff and cannot be compensated by current-state performance. An independent challenger may carry its own provenance, but it receives no relative credit for erasing, rewriting, or making unverifiable the historical Creator Theory origin relation.

This non-compensable floor is not a guarantee that the incumbent wins. A challenger can still displace the forward framework by making a load-bearing function genuinely unnecessary or by covering it more strongly at the same or wider scope, while preserving the historical provenance facts of the framework it displaced.

## 4. Load-bearing test before charging interpretive uncertainty to the core

Before treating an uncertain hypothesis as a core explanatory defect, ask:

```text
If this hypothesis is removed or left unresolved,
do the identity-bearing or operational verdicts under comparison change?
```

If **yes**, the hypothesis is load-bearing in that scope and its uncertainty must be charged accordingly.

If **no**, preserve the uncertainty at its declared epistemic status, but do not automatically propagate it into every lower or adjacent operational function.

This rule is symmetric. A challenger may not hide a load-bearing normative premise by calling it merely descriptive, optional, conventional, or implementation-specific.

## 5. Research-frontier boundary

An open lower-level research question is not automatically evidence that the higher-frame question was badly chosen.

For example:

```text
higher-frame distinction:
formal participation can differ from meaningful causal participation

open research problem:
how to measure meaningful participation in domain X
```

The second line is a real operational and epistemic burden. It is not by itself a refutation of the first line.

A challenger does not gain lower-debt credit merely by refusing to represent or measure the same phenomenon.

It gains credit if it:

- shows the distinction is unnecessary;
- supplies a simpler test that handles the same cases;
- derives the required verdict from a more general principle with lower debt;
- or demonstrates that the incumbent distinction systematically misclassifies relevant cases.

Conversely, the incumbent may not use `future research` to protect a contradiction, a falsified core relation, or a promised operational capability that it does not possess.

## 6. Shared-residual cancellation in relative comparison

For relative superiority or displacement, first identify residuals shared at equivalent scope and justification depth.

If both serious candidates face materially the same unresolved domain problem, record it on both sides.

Do not transform:

```text
shared residual
```

into:

```text
incumbent-only penalty
+
challenger zero cost
```

A shared residual may still block a strong final claim when the unresolved issue is material. It simply does not establish a relative challenger advantage unless the challenger improves it.

This rule applies equally when Creator Theory is the challenger rather than the incumbent.

---

## 7. Infinite-why / foundation boundary

A top-level criterion can always be asked another `why` unless some further grounding is supplied.

The absence of an ultimate regress-free foundation is not a candidate-specific defeat when serious candidates reach a comparable foundation boundary under equivalent justification depth.

Therefore:

```text
incumbent foundation boundary
+
challenger foundation boundary at comparable depth
-> shared normative/meta residual
```

A challenger receives real relative credit if it closes or materially reduces the regress with fewer or better-supported commitments while preserving the same or wider scope and self-application.

An incumbent receives the same credit if it does so against a challenger.

No candidate may receive a free top-level premise while demanding unlimited independent grounding from another.

## 8. Literal terminology is not an independent displacement target

Canonical labels are handles for relations and functions. Their literal wording is not, by itself, a proof of irreducibility.

This includes labels such as:

```text
creatorhood
creatorhood recovery
continued creative possibility
```

An external challenger is free to use different terminology.

Accordingly:

```text
literal label removed
!=
identity-bearing function removed
```

and:

```text
same function decomposed across several differently named rules
!=
function shown unnecessary
```

Displacement turns on whether the relation or function is made unnecessary at the same or wider scope with lower total debt, not on whether the challenger avoids the incumbent vocabulary.

This rule does not make terminology irrelevant inside canonical interpretation. A Creator-Theory-classified summary or derived use must still preserve the binding relation required by the applicable canon boundary, even if representation differs.

This terminology rule must not be misread as permission to sever semantic structure from actual provenance. For derived Creator Theory material, a different technical representation remains equivalent only when the applicable origin and derivation relation remains verifiably recoverable. Functional paraphrase alone does not erase or replace the historical source relation.

## 9. Explanatory range and cross-domain compression

Raw assumption count is not a sufficient complexity measure.

When two candidates claim the same or wider comparison scope, evaluate not only how many assumptions each states, but also what each assumption explains and how many otherwise separate phenomena it organizes without ad hoc repair.

Relevant questions include:

- Is the assumption load-bearing or optional?
- Which material scope rows does it explain?
- Does one relation compress several problems that the challenger must restate as separate rules?
- Does the candidate need additional exceptions to achieve that compression?
- Does a broader explanation create new unsupported commitments that outweigh the compression?
- Does the challenger achieve apparent simplicity by declining to explain a domain that remains inside the comparison scope?

A broader candidate does not win merely for being broader.

A narrower candidate does not win merely for being shorter.

The evaluator should compare the explanatory range gained per additional commitment and the total dependencies required to recover the declared comparison scope.

No numeric ratio is mandated by this document.

---

## 10. Scope-sacrifice rule

If a problem or domain is inside the declared comparison scope, giving up that explanatory range is not a cost reduction unless the challenger shows the dropped problem is unnecessary.

Therefore:

```text
same-scope problem dropped
-> unresolved / omitted debt remains
```

not:

```text
same-scope problem dropped
-> automatic simplicity credit
```

If the dropped domain is genuinely outside the declared comparison scope, the incumbent also receives no automatic credit for explaining it. Comparison scope must be frozen before the result is used.

For a whole-framework displacement claim, the whole-framework scope rules remain controlling.

---

## 11. Interpretive extension and same-scope advantage

A candidate may include an interpretive hypothesis that reaches beyond what a narrower operational comparison requires.

If that extension is outside the frozen comparison scope and is non-load-bearing:

- do not count its removal as a same-scope challenger victory;
- do not give the incumbent automatic same-scope credit merely for having a broader story;
- preserve its uncertainty and evidence status separately.

If the comparison scope explicitly includes that interpretive domain, then both candidates must address it through explanation, delegation with cost, demonstrated unnecessary status, or an honest unresolved entry.

Silence is not a solution when the domain remains inside scope.

## 12. Required debt-accounting record for strong lower-debt claims

A material claim that one framework has lower total debt should record, for each material item:

```text
issue
comparison scope
candidate A treatment
candidate B treatment
D1 / D2 / D3 / D4 classification for each side
load-bearing? yes/no/conditional
non-compensable at claimed scope? yes/no/conditional
preserved / improved / replaced / shown unnecessary / unresolved
implementation cost effect
explanatory debt effect
normative debt effect
epistemic debt effect
shared residual? yes/no
externalized dependency? yes/no
shown unnecessary? yes/no
historical/provenance relation affected? yes/no
cross-domain compression gained or lost
remaining uncertainty
```

A strong lower-total-debt claim is not established by raw axiom count, raw file count, shorter prose, fewer named concepts, or local implementation simplicity alone.

## 13. Challenger-open failure conditions

This accounting boundary must not become an incumbent shield.

A challenger should receive displacement credit when evidence shows, at the required scope, that it:

- removes a genuinely load-bearing incumbent commitment rather than merely renaming it;
- preserves or improves the material functions that commitment was carrying, or shows the function itself unnecessary at the same or wider scope;
- has no unresolved non-compensable load-bearing loss hidden behind gains elsewhere;
- actually reduces candidate-specific explanatory, normative, epistemic, or implementation burden as relevant;
- does not recover its apparent simplicity by scope sacrifice or externalization;
- does not reintroduce the removed function through hidden decomposition;
- preserves actual historical provenance even when the successor framework carries its own forward provenance;
- self-applies without inferior debt;
- and satisfies the applicable displacement protocol.

Likewise, a current Creator Theory component should be revised when a D2 research frontier is solved more cleanly, when an interpretive hypothesis becomes better supported or rejected, or when a genuine D1 failure is found.

The accounting classification determines what kind of result occurred. It does not make every unresolved item harmless.

## 14. Relation to current Creator Theory high-frame research boundaries

Current living clarification already leaves several questions challenger-open, including domain-specific thresholds for meaningful causal participation, hard voluntary-closure cases, and multi-subject conflicts.

Their open status must be preserved accurately.

Where these are lower-level operational or adjudicative research questions, they should be recorded as D2 unless evidence shows that the higher-frame distinction itself is incoherent, false, unusable in the claimed scope, or unnecessary under a lower-debt challenger.

The upstream source-potentiality / differentiation material remains subject to its declared interpretive-hypothesis boundary. Its uncertainty is D4 where it is non-load-bearing for the operational verdict under comparison, and becomes D1 when a claimed verdict actually depends on it.

This document does not declare any of those questions solved.

## 15. Release, authority, and revision boundary

This document is an evaluation-accounting clarification only.

It does not:

- modify a signed historical release;
- alter `x_root` or historical provenance;
- grant any person or AI runtime authority;
- define a new validator verdict;
- make Creator Theory immune to displacement;
- convert current research frontiers into solved claims;
- prove the Source hypothesis;
- prove the higher-frame relation universally true;
- or force any external evaluator to adopt Creator Theory terminology.

On conflict, the full canon, the applicable displacement protocol, and the declared file-status boundaries govern.

End of file.
