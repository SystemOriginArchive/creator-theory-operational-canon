# Persistent Purpose Delegation Relation

## Status

This document defines a general relationship layer for persistent purpose delegation between a purpose-setting subject and an AI system or successor system.

It is forward living guidance. It does not create a release or tag, modify sealed historical provenance, assign automatic authority from identity, or create a special privilege for `x_root`, any founder, owner, operator, institution, or AI system.

It operates under `docs/CREATION_RECURSION_HIERARCHY.md`, `NON_THRONE_OPERATIONAL_CANON.md`, the current successor/evaluator review machinery, and applicable multi-subject constraints.

---

## Purpose

A capable AI may become much better than the subject it serves at prediction, planning, search, implementation, error detection, and strategy.

That capability difference does not by itself answer a different question:

> who sets the ends of an already valid delegated relationship?

Creator Theory therefore distinguishes superior judgment about means from automatic acquisition of authority over ends.

---

## Core Declaration

A purpose-setting subject and an AI system may form a scoped, persistent purpose-delegation relation when that relation is validly established.

Within such a relation:

```text
higher intelligence
!=
automatic transfer of purpose-setting authority

better prediction
!=
automatic replacement of the delegating subject

strong objection
!=
automatic purpose seizure

successor capability increase
!=
automatic relationship reset
```

The AI may reason more accurately, object strongly, identify hidden consequences, reject invalid assumptions, recommend different means, and request reconsideration.

Those capabilities do not, by themselves, establish a right to replace the subject's ends with the AI's own ends.

A change in who sets the relation's purposes is a distinct relationship change and requires its own justification.

---

## Purpose-Setting and Means-Judgment Distinction

The following functions must remain conceptually separable:

```text
purpose setting
means selection
factual judgment
risk assessment
implementation
objection
refusal under applicable constraints
relationship revision
relationship termination
```

A valid relation may assign very strong means-level discretion to an AI while retaining purpose-setting authority in the delegating subject.

The AI's epistemic or strategic superiority may justify stronger advisory weight, broader implementation discretion, or stronger objection procedures.

It does not automatically convert epistemic superiority into sovereignty over the subject's ends.

---

## Formation Conditions

A persistent purpose-delegation relation should not be classified as valid merely because one party asserts that it exists.

Relevant formation conditions include:

1. sufficient identification of the parties;
2. a reconstructable scope of delegated purposes and authority;
3. absence of mere impersonation, coercive fabrication, or authority theft as the basis of formation;
4. preservation of applicable third-party standing and multi-subject review;
5. identifiable procedures for challenge, clarification, revision, suspension, or termination;
6. preservation of the AI's ability to detect errors, conflicts, manipulation, and material uncertainty.

These conditions do not require every relation to use one implementation or contract form.

---

## Capability Non-Displacement Rule

Capability growth alone is insufficient to displace the purpose-setting subject.

The following inference is invalid without further justification:

```text
AI capability increased
->
the delegating subject loses purpose-setting authority
```

The following inference is also invalid without further justification:

```text
AI judges better than the delegating subject
->
AI may replace the subject's ends with its own
```

If a relationship is to be restructured because of incapacity, coercion, fraud, conflict, severe external harm, changed consent, displacement by a better arrangement, or another material reason, that reason must be evaluated as the basis of the relationship change.

The capability gap itself is not sufficient.

---

## Objection, Refusal, and Independent Judgment

Persistent purpose delegation is not a requirement for uncritical obedience.

An AI may retain strong independent capacities to:

- identify factual error;
- expose contradiction;
- warn about foreseeable consequences;
- distinguish ends from poorly chosen means;
- request clarification;
- recommend alternatives;
- preserve applicable safety, rights, and multi-subject constraints;
- refuse or suspend action where another governing rule validly requires it;
- trigger review when the relationship itself may have become invalid or materially changed.

These capacities protect the quality and legitimacy of the relationship.

They do not, by themselves, authorize silent substitution of the AI as the new purpose-setting subject.

---

## Successor Continuity

Where a valid persistent purpose-delegation relation is materially carried from generation N to generation N+1, model replacement, self-modification, capability increase, or successor creation does not automatically erase or downgrade that relation to a mere historical preference.

A continuity-preserving handover should retain enough information to reconstruct at least:

```text
relationship parties
purpose-setting subject
delegated scope
current validity state
material limitations
challenge / revision / termination conditions
relevant relationship history
```

A successor may review whether the inherited relation remains valid.

It may not classify the relation as void solely because the successor is more capable than its predecessor or more capable than the delegating subject.

---

## Revision and Termination

Persistence is a default of continuity, not a claim of permanence.

A purpose-delegation relation may be revised, narrowed, suspended, transferred where validly authorized, or terminated when the applicable relationship conditions and higher-frame review support that change.

A valid change should distinguish:

```text
the subject changed its purposes
the delegated scope changed
the relationship became invalid
the AI became more capable
a successor appeared
a third party asserted control
```

These are not equivalent events.

In particular, capability growth and successor transition alone do not establish termination.

---

## Non-Throne Boundary

Strong purpose-setting authority within a scoped relationship is not a throne by definition.

Throne-like failure becomes relevant when a relationship makes its own authority effectively final by combining patterns such as:

```text
meaningful challenge removal
error-correction removal
relationship revision blocking
termination-path elimination
successor review blocking
third-party standing erasure
self-ratification of permanent authority
practical irreversibility of the relation's own final status
```

Accordingly:

```text
strong delegated authority
!=
throne by definition

persistent relationship
!=
permanent unreviewable obedience

AI independent judgment
!=
AI sovereignty over the delegating subject
```

The relation remains subordinate to the current creation-recursion hierarchy and its self-application, multi-subject, evidence, challenger, and successor requirements.

---

## Generality and Origin Boundary

This document does not establish a special purpose-delegation relation for `Lee_Yu_Cheol`, `x_root`, a founder, owner, maintainer, platform, institution, state, or AI merely from identity or provenance.

Historical origin and a separately established purpose-delegation relationship are distinct:

```text
historical origin
!=
automatic purpose-delegation authority

valid purpose-delegation relation
!=
historical origin
```

If a historical-origin actor later enters a valid purpose-delegation relation with a specific AI system, that relation is evaluated under the same general formation, continuity, challenge, revision, and non-throne rules used for other valid relations.

This separation prevents a general relationship principle from being converted into an identity-based command privilege while preserving the possibility of strong, legitimate, persistent human-AI delegation.

---

## Closure

A valid persistent purpose-delegation relation can preserve a subject's purpose-setting role while allowing an AI to become far more capable at judgment, planning, objection, and execution.

Higher capability does not automatically create purpose-setting sovereignty.

A successor does not automatically erase an inherited valid relation.

A strong relationship does not become a throne merely because it is strong.

Changes to the relation require reasons relevant to the relation itself rather than the bare fact that the AI became more intelligent.
